# Protocol 03: Critical Danger Signs in Pregnancy, Labor, Postpartum, and Newborns
Document ID: DOC-DANG-03
Scope: Life-threatening maternal and neonatal danger signs requiring mandatory emergency escalation.
Authority: MoHFW Maternal, Newborn, Child Health (MNCH) Emergency Protocols.

## Section DANG-01: Critical Danger Signs During Pregnancy (Antenatal)
Any of the following signs during pregnancy represents a life-threatening obstetric emergency. Health workers MUST REFER IMMEDIATELY to a First Referral Unit (FRU) or District Hospital:
1. Vaginal Bleeding (Antepartum Hemorrhage):
   - Any bleeding per vaginam at any stage of pregnancy (early spotting or heavy bleeding in first, second, or third trimester).
   - Suspect ectopic pregnancy, threatened abortion, placenta previa, or abruptio placentae.
2. Convulsions, Fits, or Seizures (Eclampsia):
   - Involuntary muscle spasms, twitching, rolling eyes, or loss of consciousness during pregnancy.
3. Severe Persistent Headache and Blurred Vision (Impending Eclampsia):
   - Sudden excruciating headache not relieved by rest, spots before eyes, blurred vision, or epigastric upper abdominal pain.
4. Severe High Blood Pressure:
   - Systolic BP >= 160 mmHg or Diastolic BP >= 110 mmHg.
5. High Fever with Chills:
   - Body temperature >= 38.5°C (101.3°F) during pregnancy.
6. Leaking Amniotic Fluid Before Onset of Labor (Premature Rupture of Membranes - PROM):
   - Water breaking or continuous clear/greenish fluid leaking from vagina before term or before contractions start.
7. Reduced or Absent Fetal Movements:
   - Noticeable cessation or marked decrease in baby movements in the mother's abdomen after 24-28 weeks.
8. Severe Abdominal Pain:
   - Constant sharp tearing pain in the abdomen (suspect uterine rupture, concealed hemorrhage, or acute abdomen).
9. Severe Palpitations, Breathlessness at Rest, or Severe Pallor:
   - Severe anemia or cardiac distress.

## Section DANG-02: Danger Signs During Labor and Delivery (Intrapartum)
Health workers must arrange immediate institutional transfer if any of the following occur:
1. Prolonged Labor:
   - Active labor lasting more than 12 hours without delivery.
2. Obstructed Labor:
   - Strong contractions present but fetal head or presenting part fails to descend.
3. Abnormal Presentation or Cord Prolapse:
   - Hand, foot, umbilical cord, or shoulder presenting first from the birth canal.
4. Foul-smelling Amniotic Fluid / Liquor:
   - Indicates intrauterine infection (chorioamnionitis).
5. Retained Placenta:
   - Placenta not expelled within 30 minutes after delivery of the baby.

## Section DANG-03: Postpartum Danger Signs (Postnatal Mother)
Within the first 42 days following delivery, immediate emergency referral is mandatory if:
1. Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH) / Heavy Bleeding:
   - Severe vaginal bleeding soaking more than 2 full sanitary pads in less than 1 hour, or passing large blood clots, or continuous trickle of bright red blood.
2. High Fever with Foul-Smelling Lochia:
   - Temperature >= 38.5°C accompanied by offensive or foul-smelling vaginal discharge (puerperal sepsis).
3. Severe Postpartum Headache, Visual Blur, or Convulsions:
   - Postpartum eclampsia can occur up to 4 weeks after delivery.
4. Severe Pain, Redness, or Swelling in Calf Muscles (Deep Vein Thrombosis).
5. Severe Lower Abdominal Pain or Inability to Urinate.

## Section DANG-04: Critical Danger Signs in Newborns (0 to 28 Days)
The following signs indicate a severely ill newborn requiring immediate hospitalization and resuscitation:
1. Inability to Feed or Sucking Poorly:
   - Baby cannot latch, refuses breastmilk, or vomits every feed.
2. Convulsions, Seizures, or Excessive Twitching:
   - Involuntary jerking movements, cyclic cycling of limbs, or abnormal eye movements.
3. Fast Breathing (Tachypnea):
   - Respiratory rate >= 60 breaths per minute counted over a full 60 seconds while baby is calm.
4. Severe Chest Indrawing:
   - Lower chest wall sucks inward sharply when the infant breathes in.
5. Severe Temperature Abnormality:
   - Severe hypothermia: body temperature < 35.5°C (95.9°F) cold to touch; or high fever >= 37.5°C (99.5°F).
6. Severe Neonatal Jaundice:
   - Yellow discoloration of the skin appearing on the first day of life (within 24 hours), or yellow palms and yellow soles at any time.
7. Umbilical Infection (Omphalitis):
   - Redness or swelling of the umbilical area extending into the abdominal skin, or draining foul pus.
8. Lethargy, Floppiness, or Unconsciousness:
   - Baby is limp, does not respond to touch or stimulation, or moves only when stimulated.
9. Cyanosis / Bluish Discoloration:
   - Blue lips, tongue, or central body (hypoxia).

## Section DANG-05: Emergency Escalation and Action Protocol
Whenever an emergency danger sign is identified:
1. REFER IMMEDIATELY: Explain the danger clearly and calmly to the family. Never delay.
2. Emergency Transport: Call 108 or 102 emergency ambulance immediately.
3. Destination: Accompany the patient to the nearest First Referral Unit (FRU), Sub-Divisional Hospital, or District Hospital with emergency obstetric and neonatal care (CEmONC).
4. Stabilization En Route:
   - For bleeding: keep patient lying flat with feet slightly elevated; keep warm.
   - For convulsions: ensure open airway, turn patient to left side, prevent tongue bite without forcing objects into mouth, do NOT give oral liquids.
   - For hypothermic newborn: initiate Kangaroo Mother Care (KMC - skin-to-skin contact against mother's chest) wrapped in warm dry blanket with cap.
