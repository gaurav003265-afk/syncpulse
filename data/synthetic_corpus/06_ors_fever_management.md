# Protocol 06: Diarrhea, Dehydration Assessment, ORS Preparation, and Fever Management
Document ID: DOC-ORS-06
Scope: Frontline childhood diarrhea assessment, zinc supplementation, ORS, fever signs, and safe home care.
Authority: MoHFW National Guidelines for Integrated Management of Neonatal and Childhood Illness (IMNCI).

## Section ORS-01: Dehydration Classification in Diarrheal Disease
In every child presenting with loose stools / diarrhea, evaluate hydration status immediately:
1. Classification Categories:
   - Category A: No Dehydration. Child is alert, active, eyes normal, tears present, drinks normally, skin pinch on abdomen goes back immediately (< 1 second).
   - Category B: Some Dehydration. Child is restless, irritable, eyes sunken, drinks eagerly / thirsty, skin pinch goes back slowly (between 1 and 2 seconds).
   - Category C: Severe Dehydration (EMERGENCY). Child is lethargic, limp, floppy, or unconscious; eyes very sunken and dry; unable to drink or drinking poorly; skin pinch goes back very slowly (> 2 seconds).
2. Action for Severe Dehydration:
   - IMMEDIATE REFERRAL to nearest hospital or CHC for intravenous (IV) Ringer's Lactate resuscitation. Start giving ORS sips en route if child can swallow.

## Section ORS-02: Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS) Preparation and Dosage
1. Preparation Instructions:
   - Wash hands thoroughly with soap and clean water.
   - Measure exactly 1 liter (approximately 5 standard glasses of 200 ml each) of clean boiled and cooled drinking water into a clean container.
   - Empty the entire contents of 1 packet of WHO-recommended reduced-osmolarity ORS (20.5 grams) into the water.
   - Stir well with a clean spoon until all the powder is completely dissolved.
   - Never boil the prepared ORS solution.
   - Discard any leftover ORS solution after 24 hours and prepare a fresh solution.
2. Administration and Dosage:
   - Children under 2 years: Give 50 to 100 ml (quarter to half cup) of prepared ORS after every loose stool.
   - Children 2 to 10 years: Give 100 to 200 ml (half to full cup) of prepared ORS after every loose stool.
   - Older children and adults: Give as much ORS fluid as they can drink.
   - If the child vomits, wait 10 minutes, then resume offering ORS slowly, one teaspoon every 1-2 minutes.

## Section ORS-03: Zinc Supplementation Protocol
Zinc is mandatory along with ORS for all children suffering from diarrhea aged 2 months to 5 years:
1. Dosage and Duration:
   - Age 2 months to 6 months: 10 mg elemental zinc once daily for 14 continuous days.
   - Age 6 months to 5 years: 20 mg elemental zinc once daily for 14 continuous days.
2. Method of Administration:
   - Dispersible zinc tablet: Dissolve the tablet in a small spoon with expressed breastmilk or clean water.
   - Critical Instruction: The full 14-day course must be completed even after diarrhea stops to rebuild intestinal lining and boost immunity against recurrent episodes.

## Section ORS-04: Red Flag Danger Signs in Diarrhea Requiring Immediate Escalation
Do not treat at home; refer immediately to a medical facility if the child has:
1. Blood or mucus in the stool (Dysentery).
2. Lethargy, unconsciousness, floppiness, or inability to drink.
3. Persistent loose watery diarrhea lasting 14 days or longer (Persistent Diarrhea).
4. High fever (>= 38.5°C / 101.3°F) accompanied by diarrhea.
5. Inability to keep any liquids down (persistent vomiting).
6. Very slow skin pinch (> 2 seconds).

## Section ORS-05: Fever Assessment and Emergency Red Flags
Measure body temperature using a digital thermometer placed in the axilla (armpit) for 1 minute:
- Normal body temperature: 36.5°C to 37.5°C (97.7°F to 99.5°F).
- Fever: Axillary temperature >= 37.5°C (99.5°F).
- High Fever: >= 38.5°C (101.3°F).
Immediate Emergency Referral is mandatory if fever is accompanied by ANY of the following red flags:
1. Fever persisting for more than 7 days without diagnosis.
2. Stiff neck, inability to bend neck forward to touch chin to chest, or arching of back (meningitis sign).
3. Convulsions, seizures, tremors, or loss of consciousness.
4. Altered sensorium, severe drowsiness, confusion, or delirium.
5. Petechial rash, dark purple hemorrhagic spots, bleeding from gums, nose, or vomit (suspected severe Dengue or Meningococcal disease).
6. Severe respiratory distress: fast breathing (>= 50 breaths/min for 2-11 months; >= 40 breaths/min for 12-59 months) or chest indrawing.

## Section ORS-06: Safe Home Fever Management and Paracetamol Guidance
For uncomplicated fever without any red flag danger signs:
1. Physical Cooling:
   - Keep the patient in a well-ventilated, shaded room.
   - Dress in light cotton clothing; do not wrap tightly in heavy blankets.
   - Tepid sponging: Use cloth soaked in room-temperature water on forehead, armpits, and groin. Never use cold or ice water, which causes shivering and raises core temperature.
2. Hydration and Feeding:
   - Offer frequent fluids: boiled cooled water, lemon water, buttermilk, light soups, ORS, or breastmilk.
   - Continue feeding small frequent meals.
3. Medication:
   - Paracetamol syrup or tablet may be given per age-appropriate national protocol dosage chart for high temperature or body ache.
   - Never administer Aspirin or Ibuprofen to children (risk of Reye's syndrome or severe bleeding in dengue).
   - If fever persists beyond 3 days or red flags appear, consult the PHC Medical Officer immediately.
