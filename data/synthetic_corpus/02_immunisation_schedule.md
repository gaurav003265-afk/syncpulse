# Protocol 02: National Immunisation Schedule (NIS) Guidelines
Document ID: DOC-IMM-02
Scope: Frontline childhood and maternal immunisation schedule, routes, doses, and AEFI handling.
Authority: MoHFW Universal Immunization Programme (UIP) India Guidelines.

## Section IMM-01: At Birth Immunisation (Within 24-48 Hours)
Every newborn must receive three critical vaccines immediately after birth, preferably before discharge from the health facility:
1. BCG (Bacille Calmette-Guerin):
   - Protection: Against severe childhood tuberculosis (TB meningitis and miliary TB).
   - Dose and Route: 0.05 ml intradermal on left upper arm if under 1 month; 0.1 ml if over 1 month up to 1 year of age.
   - Normal reaction: Small red papule appears at 2-3 weeks, turns into a vesicle, breaks and forms a permanent scar at 6-8 weeks. Do not rub, apply ointment or press.
2. OPV-0 (Oral Polio Vaccine Birth Dose):
   - Protection: Against poliomyelitis.
   - Dose and Route: 2 drops orally. Must be administered within the first 15 days of birth.
3. Hepatitis B Birth Dose:
   - Protection: Prevents perinatal transmission of Hepatitis B virus.
   - Dose and Route: 0.5 ml intramuscular (IM) on anterolateral aspect of mid-thigh.
   - Critical Timing: Must be administered within 24 hours of birth. If missed past 24 hours, do not give birth dose; start Hepatitis B containing pentavalent at 6 weeks.

## Section IMM-02: Primary Infant Immunisation at 6, 10, and 14 Weeks
1. At 6 Weeks:
   - Pentavalent-1 (Diphtheria, Pertussis, Tetanus, Hepatitis B, Hib): 0.5 ml IM, anterolateral mid-thigh.
   - OPV-1: 2 drops orally.
   - Rotavirus Vaccine (RVV-1): 5 drops orally (or 2.5 ml depending on presentation). Protects against rotavirus severe diarrhea.
   - Fractional Inactivated Polio Vaccine (fIPV-1): 0.1 ml intradermal, right upper arm.
   - Pneumococcal Conjugate Vaccine (PCV-1): 0.5 ml IM, anterolateral mid-thigh.
2. At 10 Weeks:
   - Pentavalent-2: 0.5 ml IM, anterolateral mid-thigh.
   - OPV-2: 2 drops orally.
   - Rotavirus Vaccine (RVV-2): 5 drops orally.
3. At 14 Weeks:
   - Pentavalent-3: 0.5 ml IM, anterolateral mid-thigh.
   - OPV-3: 2 drops orally.
   - Rotavirus Vaccine (RVV-3): 5 drops orally.
   - Fractional IPV-2 (fIPV-2): 0.1 ml intradermal, right upper arm.
   - Pneumococcal Conjugate Vaccine (PCV-2): 0.5 ml IM, anterolateral mid-thigh.

## Section IMM-03: Infant Immunisation at 9 to 12 Months
1. Measles and Rubella (MR-1):
   - Protection: Against measles and congenital rubella infection.
   - Dose and Route: 0.5 ml subcutaneous (SC), right upper arm. Given completed 9 months up to 12 months.
2. PCV Booster:
   - Dose and Route: 0.5 ml IM, anterolateral mid-thigh. Given at 9 completed months along with MR-1.
3. Japanese Encephalitis (JE-1):
   - Given only in endemic districts. 0.5 ml SC, left upper arm.
4. Vitamin A 1st Dose:
   - Dose and Route: 1 ml (100,000 International Units) orally with spoon. Administered alongside MR-1 at 9 months. Prevents night blindness and boosts immune defense.

## Section IMM-04: Toddler Immunisation at 16 to 24 Months
1. MR 2nd Dose (MR-2):
   - Dose and Route: 0.5 ml subcutaneous, right upper arm.
2. DPT First Booster (DPT Booster-1):
   - Protection: Boosts immunity against Diphtheria, Pertussis, and Tetanus.
   - Dose and Route: 0.5 ml IM, anterolateral aspect of mid-thigh.
3. OPV Booster:
   - Dose and Route: 2 drops orally.
4. Japanese Encephalitis 2nd Dose (JE-2):
   - In endemic districts: 0.5 ml SC, left upper arm.
5. Vitamin A 2nd Dose:
   - Dose and Route: 2 ml (200,000 International Units) orally. Subsequent doses (doses 3 to 9) are given every 6 months until age 5.

## Section IMM-05: Child Immunisation at 5-6 Years and Adolescence (10 & 16 Years)
1. At 5 to 6 Years:
   - DPT Second Booster (DPT Booster-2): 0.5 ml IM on upper arm (deltoid muscle).
2. At 10 Years:
   - Td (Tetanus and Adult Diphtheria): 0.5 ml IM on upper arm (deltoid muscle).
3. At 16 Years:
   - Td Booster: 0.5 ml IM on upper arm (deltoid muscle).

## Section IMM-06: Minor Reactions vs Adverse Events Following Immunisation (AEFI)
1. Minor Expected Reactions:
   - Mild fever (below 38.5°C / 101.3°F), mild local swelling, redness, and pain at the injection site.
   - Advice: Reassure mother. Apply cool cloth / compress to injection site. Continue breastfeeding. Paracetamol drops may be given if advised. Do not apply ice directly, mud, cow dung, or traditional herbal pastes.
2. Serious AEFI (Red Flag Medical Emergencies):
   - High persistent fever >= 39°C (102.2°F).
   - Inconsolable persistent screaming or crying lasting more than 3 hours.
   - Seizures, fits, or convulsions occurring within 48-72 hours of vaccination.
   - Anaphylaxis or severe allergic shock: sudden facial swelling, breathing difficulty, wheezing, pallor, or collapse within 30 minutes of vaccination.
   - Abscess or extensive severe swelling spreading across the joint.
   - Immediate action: Administer emergency first aid if trained (Adrenaline for anaphylaxis by medical staff), keep airway clear, and IMMEDIATELY transport to CHC/District Hospital. Report to Medical Officer within 24 hours.
