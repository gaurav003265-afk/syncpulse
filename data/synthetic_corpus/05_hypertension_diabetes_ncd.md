# Protocol 05: Non-Communicable Diseases (NCD): Hypertension and Diabetes Screening and Follow-Up
Document ID: DOC-NCD-05
Scope: Community screening of adults >= 30 years, CBAC form, follow-up, and emergency danger signs.
Authority: MoHFW National Programme for Prevention and Control of Cancer, Diabetes, CVD and Stroke (NPCDCS).

## Section NCD-01: Blood Pressure Measurement and Classification
Every adult aged 30 years and above must be screened annually for hypertension using a validated digital sphygmomanometer:
1. Measurement Technique:
   - Person must be seated quietly for at least 5 minutes before measurement.
   - Feet flat on floor, arm supported at heart level, no smoking or caffeine 30 minutes prior.
   - Take 2 readings 2 minutes apart and record the average.
2. Blood Pressure Classification:
   - Normal Blood Pressure: Systolic BP < 120 mmHg AND Diastolic BP < 80 mmHg.
   - Pre-Hypertension: Systolic BP 120-139 mmHg OR Diastolic BP 80-89 mmHg.
   - Stage 1 Hypertension: Systolic BP 140-159 mmHg OR Diastolic BP 90-99 mmHg.
   - Stage 2 Hypertension: Systolic BP >= 160 mmHg OR Diastolic BP >= 100 mmHg.
Any individual with BP >= 140/90 mmHg on two separate visits must be referred to Primary Health Centre (PHC) Medical Officer for clinical confirmation and prescription.

## Section NCD-02: Hypertensive Crisis and Emergency Danger Signs
Immediate emergency referral to Community Health Centre (CHC) or District Hospital is mandatory if:
1. Hypertensive Crisis:
   - Extremely high blood pressure: Systolic BP >= 180 mmHg OR Diastolic BP >= 110 mmHg.
2. Red-Flag Symptoms Associated with High BP (Target Organ Damage):
   - Sudden severe, throbbing headache or occipital pain.
   - Sudden loss of vision, double vision, or severe blurring.
   - Sudden weakness, numbness, or tingling in one side of the face, arm, or leg (suspected Stroke / TIA).
   - Slurred speech or difficulty speaking.
   - Severe chest tightness, crushing central chest pain radiating to left arm or jaw (suspected Myocardial Infarction / Heart Attack).
   - Sudden severe breathlessness, panting at rest, or coughing pink frothy sputum (suspected Acute Pulmonary Edema).
Action: Keep patient seated calmly, do not let them exert themselves, arrange emergency transport (108), and refer immediately.

## Section NCD-03: Diabetes Screening and Blood Sugar Targets
All adults >= 30 years must be screened for diabetes using a glucometer with disposable lancets and strips:
1. Blood Glucose Diagnostic Criteria:
   - Fasting Blood Sugar (FBS, after 8 hours of fasting):
     * Normal: Under 100 mg/dL.
     * Impaired Fasting Glucose (Prediabetes): 100 to 125 mg/dL.
     * Suspected Diabetes: >= 126 mg/dL (requires lab venous plasma confirmation).
   - Random Blood Sugar (RBS, anytime regardless of meals):
     * Normal: Under 140 mg/dL.
     * Impaired: 140 to 199 mg/dL.
     * Suspected Diabetes: >= 200 mg/dL accompanied by classic symptoms (increased thirst, frequent urination, unexplained weight loss).
2. Treatment Targets for Diagnosed Diabetic Patients:
   - Fasting blood sugar: 80 to 130 mg/dL.
   - Post-prandial blood sugar: Under 180 mg/dL.
   - HbA1c target: Below 7.0%.

## Section NCD-04: Acute Diabetic Emergencies (Hypoglycemia & Hyperglycemia)
1. Hypoglycemia (Low Blood Sugar < 70 mg/dL) - ACUTE LIFE THREAT:
   - Symptoms: Excessive cold sweating, trembling/shakiness, sudden extreme hunger, rapid heartbeat/palpitations, dizziness, anxiety, slurred speech, confusion.
   - Emergency Management:
     * If conscious: Give Rule of 15 -> Give 15-20 grams of fast-acting sugar (3-4 teaspoons of plain sugar in water, jaggery, honey, or 1/2 cup fruit juice). Recheck blood sugar in 15 minutes.
     * If drowsy, unconscious, or convulsing: DO NOT pour liquids or food into mouth (choking hazard). Place in recovery position on side, call 108 ambulance, and transfer immediately to hospital for IV dextrose.
2. Hyperglycemia Emergency (Diabetic Ketoacidosis / HHS):
   - Blood glucose >= 300 mg/dL, deep rapid breathing (Kussmaul breathing), fruity or acetone smell on breath, persistent vomiting, severe dehydration, altered mental state. Refer immediately.

## Section NCD-05: Routine Follow-Up and Lifestyle Counseling
Frontline health workers conduct monthly home visits for registered hypertensive and diabetic patients:
1. Medication Adherence:
   - Emphasize taking prescribed tablets every day without skipping doses even if feeling completely well.
   - Never stop or change medications without consulting the PHC doctor.
2. Dietary Counseling:
   - Salt restriction: Less than 1 level teaspoon (under 5 grams) of salt per day for the entire family. Avoid pickles, papads, and processed snacks.
   - Sugar restriction: Eliminate added white sugar, sweets, sweet tea, and sugary carbonated drinks.
   - Increase fiber: Millets (ragi, jowar, bajra), whole grains, green leafy vegetables, and salads.
3. Physical Activity:
   - At least 30 minutes of moderate aerobic exercise (brisk walking) 5 days a week.
4. Tobacco and Alcohol Cessation:
   - Strictly counsel against bidi, cigarettes, gutkha, khaini, and alcohol consumption.
