# Protocol 01: Antenatal Care (ANC) Schedule and Care Guidelines
Document ID: DOC-ANC-01
Scope: Frontline ASHA and ANM maternal health visits, monitoring schedules, and routine prophylaxis.
Authority: MoHFW National Health Mission Maternal Health Division Guidelines.

## Section ANC-01: Minimum Antenatal Visits Schedule
Every pregnant woman must receive at least 4 comprehensive antenatal check-ups during her pregnancy:
1. First ANC Visit: Within 12 weeks of pregnancy (first trimester) as soon as pregnancy is suspected or missed period occurs. Complete registration, issue MCP card, calculate Expected Date of Delivery (EDD) from Last Menstrual Period (LMP).
2. Second ANC Visit: Between 14 and 26 weeks of pregnancy (second trimester). Perform routine physical check-up, abdomen examination, and screen for maternal complications.
3. Third ANC Visit: Between 28 and 34 weeks of pregnancy (third trimester). Assess fetal growth, check blood pressure, monitor fetal heart sounds and movement.
4. Fourth ANC Visit: Between 36 weeks and delivery/term (third trimester). Confirm fetal presentation and position, review birth preparedness plan, and identify institutional delivery center.
High-risk pregnancies (severe anemia, hypertension, gestational diabetes, previous cesarean, twin pregnancy) require more frequent visits as advised by the Medical Officer.

## Section ANC-02: Routine Interventions and Prophylaxis
1. Tetanus and Adult Diphtheria (Td) Vaccination:
   - Td Dose 1: Given early in pregnancy at registration.
   - Td Dose 2: Given 4 weeks after Td-1.
   - Td Booster: If the woman received 2 Td doses or a booster in a pregnancy within the last 3 years, only 1 Td booster dose is needed for the current pregnancy.
2. Iron and Folic Acid (IFA) Supplementation:
   - Every pregnant woman must consume 1 IFA tablet daily (containing 60 mg elemental iron and 500 mcg folic acid) for at least 180 days, starting after the first trimester (from 14 weeks onwards).
   - If severe anemia is diagnosed (Hb < 7 g/dL), refer immediately to Medical Officer / Community Health Centre (CHC).
3. Calcium Supplementation:
   - 1000 mg daily (2 tablets of 500 mg calcium with 250 IU Vitamin D3) starting from 14 weeks of pregnancy until 6 months postpartum.
   - Important: Never take calcium tablets together with IFA tablets; take them at different times (e.g., calcium in morning after breakfast, iron at night after food) because calcium reduces iron absorption.
4. Albendazole Deworming:
   - 1 tablet of Albendazole 400 mg single dose given orally in the second trimester after 14 weeks.

## Section ANC-03: Routine ANC Examinations and Measurements
At each ANC check-up, the health worker must perform and record:
1. Weight recording: Normal weight gain during pregnancy is 10 to 12 kg (about 1.5 to 2 kg per month in second and third trimesters). Weight gain under 1 kg per month or sudden excessive weight gain requires investigation.
2. Blood Pressure (BP) Measurement: Normal BP is below 120/80 mmHg. Any reading with Systolic BP >= 140 mmHg or Diastolic BP >= 90 mmHg indicates hypertension in pregnancy. If SBP >= 160 or DBP >= 110, it is severe hypertension requiring urgent referral.
3. Abdominal Examination: Measure fundal height using measuring tape or finger-breadths. Check fetal heart sounds (FHS) using Doppler or fetoscope after 20-24 weeks (normal rate: 120 to 160 beats per minute).
4. Laboratory Tests: Hemoglobin test (Hb >= 11 g/dL is normal; 10-10.9 mild anemia; 7-9.9 moderate; < 7 severe anemia), urine test for albumin (protein) and sugar at each visit, blood group and Rh typing, screening for HIV, Syphilis, and Hepatitis B.

## Section ANC-04: Maternal Nutrition, Rest, and Daily Care
1. Diet Advice:
   - Eat an extra nutritious meal daily during pregnancy and lactation.
   - Include diverse local foods: green leafy vegetables (spinach, drumstick leaves), pulses, lentils, seasonal fruits, milk, eggs, nuts, and clean water.
   - Use only iodized salt to prevent mental and physical disability in the child.
   - Avoid alcohol, tobacco, smoking, and taking unprescribed indigenous medicines.
2. Rest and Physical Activity:
   - Rest at least 2 hours during the day and sleep 8 hours at night.
   - Lie on the left side to improve blood circulation and nutrient supply to the fetus.
   - Avoid strenuous physical labor, lifting heavy water containers, or long bumpy travel.

## Section ANC-05: Birth Preparedness and Institutional Delivery Planning
Frontline workers must help every family prepare a Birth Preparedness Plan:
1. Identify the designated delivery institution: Primary Health Centre (PHC), Community Health Centre (CHC), or First Referral Unit (FRU) for institutional delivery.
2. Arrange reliable emergency transportation: Note emergency ambulance helpline numbers (108 / 102).
3. Identify birth companion: Family member who will accompany the woman to the facility.
4. Prepare clean clothes, baby wrapping cloth, MCP card, Aadhaar card, and bank passbook for JSY/PMMVY cash benefits.
5. Identify blood donors in advance in case blood transfusion is required.
