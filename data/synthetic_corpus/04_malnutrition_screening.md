# Protocol 04: Identification, Screening, and Management of Child Malnutrition
Document ID: DOC-NUT-04
Scope: Community screening, anthropometry, SAM/MAM classification, and NRC referral criteria.
Authority: MoHFW National Operational Guidelines on Facility and Community Management of Children with Severe Acute Malnutrition.

## Section NUT-01: Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) Screening (Children 6-59 Months)
Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) is the gold-standard frontline screening tool for wasting and acute malnutrition in children aged 6 to 59 months:
1. Measurement Technique:
   - Use the standardized colour-coded Shakir MUAC tape.
   - Measure on the LEFT arm only.
   - Find the midpoint between the tip of the left shoulder (acromion) and the tip of the elbow (olecranon) with the arm bent at 90 degrees.
   - Straighten the arm, wrap the tape gently around the midpoint without compressing soft tissue or leaving slack, and read the window.
2. MUAC Classification:
   - RED ZONE: MUAC < 115 mm (less than 11.5 cm). Indicates Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). High risk of mortality.
   - YELLOW ZONE: MUAC 115 mm to 124 mm (11.5 cm to 12.4 cm). Indicates Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM).
   - GREEN ZONE: MUAC >= 125 mm (12.5 cm or greater). Indicates Normal nutritional status.

## Section NUT-02: Bilateral Pitting Edema Assessment (Kwashiorkor)
Bilateral pitting edema is an absolute red flag sign of severe acute malnutrition (edematous SAM / kwashiorkor):
1. Testing Method:
   - Apply gentle thumb pressure on the dorsum (top surface) of BOTH feet simultaneously for 3 full seconds.
   - Release thumbs. If a clear pit or indentation remains on BOTH feet, bilateral pitting edema is present.
   - Note: Unilateral edema (in one foot only) is not nutritional edema and may be due to injury or local infection.
2. Edema Grading:
   - Grade +: Mild edema involving feet and ankles only.
   - Grade ++: Moderate edema involving feet, lower legs, hands, or lower arms.
   - Grade +++: Severe generalized edema involving face, eyes, legs, arms, and abdomen.
3. Critical Rule: ANY child with confirmed bilateral pitting edema (+, ++, or +++) must be classified as Severe Acute Malnutrition and REFERRED IMMEDIATELY to a Nutrition Rehabilitation Centre (NRC) or hospital.

## Section NUT-03: Appetite Test and Clinical Medical Complications in SAM
Every child identified with SAM (MUAC < 115 mm) must undergo an Appetite Test before deciding between community care and facility admission:
1. Appetite Test Protocol:
   - Offer a sachet of Ready-to-Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF) in a quiet area after mother washes hands.
   - A child who eagerly consumes at least one-quarter to one-third of the RUTF sachet passes the appetite test.
   - A child who refuses to eat, eats very little, or vomits persistently FAILS the appetite test.
2. Clinical Medical Complications:
   - High fever or severe hypothermia (< 35.5°C).
   - Severe persistent vomiting or inability to keep fluids down.
   - Severe watery diarrhea with severe dehydration.
   - Lower respiratory tract infection (pneumonia) with fast breathing or chest indrawing.
   - Very pale conjunctiva / severe visible wasting / extensive open skin dermatosis.

## Section NUT-04: Mandatory Referral Criteria to Nutrition Rehabilitation Centre (NRC)
A child MUST be referred immediately to an NRC or Sub-District/District Hospital if ANY of the following exist:
1. Presence of Bilateral Pitting Edema of any grade.
2. Failed Appetite Test (child unable to consume therapeutic feed).
3. MUAC < 115 mm accompanied by ANY medical complication (pneumonia, high fever, severe dehydration).
4. Infants under 6 months of age with visible severe wasting or inability to suckle.
5. Child with SAM showing rapid weight loss or apathy/lethargy.

## Section NUT-05: Community Follow-Up for Uncomplicated MAM and SAM
For children with MAM (MUAC 115-124 mm) or uncomplicated SAM (MUAC < 115 mm with good appetite and no medical complications):
1. Support and protect continued frequent breastfeeding up to 2 years of age or beyond.
2. Counsel mothers on energy-dense complementary feeding: provide 4-5 small nutrient-rich meals daily using locally available grains, pulses, oil/ghee, eggs, mashed banana, and green vegetables.
3. Bi-weekly or weekly monitoring visits by ASHA: repeat MUAC measurement and check for emergence of danger signs.
4. Provide therapeutic supplementary nutrition through Anganwadi Centre under POSHAN Abhiyaan.
